### The three phase-1 unknowns

1. **Server WebVTT cues carry no settings.** `subtitles/5/2.vtt`: `00:00:15.005 --> 00:00:19.005` / `[DE FORCED] Schild:
   "Ausgang" (00:00:15)`, no `line`/`position`/`align`. The styled DE track (`subtitles/3`) has none either.
2. **media3 ignores `setBottomPaddingFraction` for these cues** (Google TV, first build): lift 0.241 reached native,
   and the cue stayed on the bottom edge, lower even than the default 8 % (`gtv-01-padding-fraction-ignored.png`). media3
   stacks WebVTT cues without a line as line −1 (`LINE_TYPE_NUMBER`). SubtitlePainter anchors those at the output's
   bottom edge and uses the padding fraction only for `line == DIMEN_UNSET`. **Changed:** `SubtitleLift.kt` now sets the
   SubtitleView's own bottom padding instead. It is a FrameLayout, and `CanvasSubtitleOutput` draws inside
   `height − paddingBottom` (javap of media3-ui 1.9.0). Padding = the bar's overlap with the picture + 1.5 % of the
   picture. `bottomPaddingFraction` stays media3's default.
3. **AVPlayer + `textStyleRules` + HLS WebVTT, forced cues included: works, with two limits.**
   - **Anchoring:** the line percentage places the cue's **top**, on both iPhone and Apple TV. iPhone, lift 0.329, line
     59.1 % → box top at 0.589 of the picture. Apple TV, line 61.5 % → top at 664 of 1080 px. One line is about 6 % of
     the picture height (iPhone 6.0 %, Apple TV 6.3 %). **Tuned:** `cueGapPercent` 8 → **13**, which is room for two
     lines plus about 1 %. A two-line cue on Apple TV would end at about 800 px, with the bar at 805.
   - **Limit 1, a cue already on screen keeps its place.** New rules apply only to cues drawn afterwards, whether set
     paused or playing (`iphone-03`, `iphone-04`). Selecting the same legible option again (deselect + select) redraws
     the cue in the new place (`iphone-05`; Apple TV `atv-03`; the move back to the bottom when the lift returns to 0
     while playing: `atv-04`). **Added:** `redrawCues`.
   - **Limit 2, a reselect while paused hides the cue** until playback resumes (Apple TV, `atv-02`). **Changed:** the
     redraw now waits for playback (`subtitleRedrawPending`, run from `onIsPlayingChanged`). The final iOS build moved a
     displayed cue up while playing (`iphone-06`). **Still open:** pausing within about a second after a redraw left the
     paused frame without a cue, and 1 s of play afterwards did not bring it back (`iphone-07`, `iphone-08`). The
     reselect redraw is not reliable when redraws come back to back. Next options, in order: debounce the redraw (only
     the last lift within ~300 ms); skip it when the cue ends soon; or draw the cues ourselves from
     `AVPlayerItemLegibleOutput`.
   - **Picture vs view:** `videoBounds` does not exist on tvOS (the first tvOS build failed on it). The picture rect is
     now computed from `presentationSize` and the view bounds, honouring fill (`resizeAspectFill`). It is recomputed on
     every VideoView bounds change, item change and new player. Sintel is 16:9 and fills the height on both devices, so
     picture = view height there. A letterboxed case (iPad 4:3, iPhone portrait) was **not** checked. The iPhone player
     is locked to landscape (`lockPlayerLandscape`), so iPhone portrait cannot be checked.

## Phase 4 — Q2-07 rest and Q2-12 (2026-10-07 05:51–06:20, worktree at main 49878cc)

### Q2-07: what re-applied landscape ~4 s after a close

Q2's failing close (iphone-t3-02): the window turned portrait under the player at ~1.75 s, the details were upright
from 3.25 s, and at 4.25 s the app turned back to landscape and stayed. 4.25 s is exactly when F13n phase 3's
`release()` unlocked (dismissal + up to 3 s for a portrait window + 1 s settle). So it was not VLC or
expo-libvlc-player: expo-libvlc-player has no orientation code, and the AVPlayer controller was not involved. The
cause was expo-screen-orientation's unlock (`lockAsync(DEFAULT)` → `enforceDesiredDeviceOrientation`):
1. With a stale `currentScreenOrientation` (the player's landscape), it called
   `UIDevice.current.setValue(landscapeRight, "orientation")`. iOS 27 logs that as "BUG IN CLIENT OF UIKIT: Setting
   UIDevice.orientation is not supported", but the faked device orientation stuck.
2. It then called `attemptRotationToDeviceOrientation()`. Once unlocked, UIKit followed the faked landscape "device"
   and stayed there until the real device turned (Q2: until `rotate Portrait`).

Whether the app turned back depended on timing. That is why it hit 1 of 5 closes in Q2 and showed as the "known
unlock flicker" (details sideways 0.5–0.75 s) on the others.

### Fix (decision: keep a portrait phone portrait until the device itself turns)

- **patch `expo-screen-orientation+57.0.2` (new):**
  - `ScreenOrientationRegistry.enforceDesiredDeviceOrientation`: on iOS 16+ only the geometry request and
    `setNeedsUpdateOfSupportedInterfaceOrientations`. There is no faked `UIDevice` orientation and no
    `attemptRotationToDeviceOrientation` (both stay for iOS < 16). The "BUG IN CLIENT" lines are gone from the
    UIKit log.
  - `ScreenOrientationModule`: a `streamarrDeviceOrientationDidChange` event fires on every
    `UIDevice.orientationDidChangeNotification`, also while the interface is locked. Its payload is
    `{ orientation }`; the new `ModuleOrientation.from(device:)` maps the device's landscape left to the interface's
    landscape right. A `deviceOrientationEvents` constant (`true`) lets JS feature-detect the event.
